### - Tree-Ising: Repairing Language Models with Bit-Level Ising Edits

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 78 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14210)

**초록**:

Weight-space programming, introduced in recent MIT work by Takehi et al., enables a frontier LLM agent to read a network's weights and write a repair. Their GPT-6 Astra agent corrected the reasoning loops of Qwen3.5-0.8B without gradients, using 158 agent turns, 89.7 million tokens, and 50 forward-pass requests. Our method, Tree-Ising, casts this repair as a sparse discrete optimization problem over the model's own BF16 weights. Forward statistics define an objective that weakens a loop direction, strengthens a concluding direction, and preserves the layer's output on productive tokens. Within a state-tree representation, where lossless BF16 storage acts as a special case, the admissible edits are sign flips and exponent shifts that preserve all shared group exponents and mantissas. We decompose this objective exactly into independent QUBO (Ising) problems, one per output row, which we solve on a GPU using simulated annealing or quantum-inspired simulated bifurcation. On the agent's two layers, Tree-Ising achieves 54.6% on MATH-500, outperforming the agent's edit (51.7%) under the same evaluation protocol, while the base model scores 7.9% as reported. The simulated bifurcation solver yields a statistically indistinguishable repair at 55.1%. Both solvers outperform the agent edit across all five math benchmarks, with statistically significant improvements on MATH-500 and GSM8K after correcting for multiple comparisons. Our approach modifies 13% to 17% fewer weights and reduces BF16 bit flips by a factor of 2.2 to 2.3. It requires only four forward-pass requests and zero LLM tokens, with the annealing recipe costing about $0.90 in GPU time. On Qwen3.5-2B, with layers and dose selected using only the base model, the same recipe increases greedy GSM8K accuracy from 21.6% to 69.4%. As a proof of concept, we execute an eight-variable repair subproblem using QAOA on an IBM Heron processor.

### - Few-Step Streaming Video Generation via Condition Shifting

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 78 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14033) | [Project](https://www.vidu.com/vidu-stream/avatar)

**초록**:

Few-step sampling reduces the cost of streaming video generation. Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) supervises few-step generators with a separate fake-score network, which adds training memory and computation and must track the evolving generator. We extend APEX to streaming video by using condition shifting to estimate the velocity field of the generated distribution within the generator. The original text condition selects generation, while a shifted condition selects fake estimation using the same parameters. This removes the independent fake network while retaining S2-Avatar's frozen reference teacher, Self-Replay Forcing, and perceptual regularization. We evaluate APEX on 180-second videos using three backbone evaluations per block and a one-step spatial Refiner, with a 70-case human study and a 320-case StreamAV-Bench comparison. Compared with S2-Avatar, APEX achieves higher video instruction fulfillment on the Progressive track (3.856 versus 3.831) and higher visual aesthetics across both tracks (0.641898 versus 0.632606). Human raters favor APEX most strongly on instruction following, with +30.0% net preference, followed by consistency and video quality at +4.3% each. Motion quality favors S2-Avatar, audiovisual synchronization is predominantly tied, and usability is unchanged. Separate measurements under matched training settings show 4.4% lower peak allocated GPU memory per GPU and 9.2% shorter mean iteration time than DMD.

### - World In-Context Learning for Agentic Robot Manipulation

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 66 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12885) | [Project](https://archeone-ai.github.io/RoboScript/)

**초록**:

Trained action policies write manipulation skill into network weights, tying what is learned to the distribution it is learned from. In-context learning (ICL) avoids this coupling but has not matched trained policies, because a demonstration recorded in another scene carries no feedback from the world the robot currently faces. We observe that what an agent learns in context is semantic, recording task steps and object roles rather than visual appearance, so experience gathered in one world transfers to another. Based on this insight, we propose World In-Context Learning (WICL), a paradigm in which a frozen model starts from a demonstration, rehearses the task in a world such as a simulator, and writes the feedback into its context rather than its weights. We build RoboScript, a modular infrastructure that implements WICL for agentic manipulation. From a single demonstration, RoboScript learns each task through rehearsal in simulation, freezes the learned knowledge as a Task Memory, and deploys the frozen memory on new scenes with data isolation that ensures every deployment input is available on a real robot. On all 50 tasks of RoboTwin 2.0, RoboScript reaches 83.4% mean success across clean and randomized settings with one demonstration per task and no gradient updates, exceeding the strongest trained action policy by over 20 points. The method generalizes across visual distributions, improves through reflection, scales with the foundation model, and transfers to a physical dual-arm robot through cross-embodiment memory adaptation. With a world in the loop, a robot can learn a manipulation task in its context as well as it learns one in its weights. All code is available at https://github.com/ArcheOne-AI/RoboScript

### - Don't Just Trust Your Memory: Evidence-Linked Memory Reduces Memory-Induced Degradation in LLM Agents

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 61 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14320) | [Project](https://yukyeongleee.github.io/wikitrace/)

**초록**:

External memory enables LLM agents to improve from experience by distilling trajectories of past tasks into concise lessons for new tasks. However, memory can also lead to memory-induced degradation, where an agent fails on tasks it would solve without memory, often because it follows a lesson that is incorrect, or misapplies or misreads a valid one. This is because lessons are inferred from trajectories labeled only with task success or failure, yet are typically provided without the trajectories they came from, leaving the agent no way to check them. To address this, we introduce WikiTrace, an evidence-linked memory system designed to let agents check each lesson against its original trajectory. Specifically, motivated by the finding that a trajectory often still holds the evidence needed to catch an erroneous lesson, WikiTrace stores each lesson as a concise insight linked to a summary of why its trajectory succeeded or failed and to the raw trajectory itself. Then, on a new task, the agent starts from all insights and, whenever one lacks details needed to act, conflicts with another, or comes from a failed task, descends through its links with terminal tools to fill in missing details or correct the insight before acting. We validate WikiTrace on five agentic benchmarks, showing that it consistently outperforms existing memory approaches and a baseline given the same insights alone, while also reducing memory-induced degradation. Our code is available at https://github.com/yukyeongleee/wikitrace

### - dNaViT: Discrete Codes are General-Purpose Visual Representations

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 33 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12426) | [Project](https://lixiaotong97.github.io/dNaViT/)

**초록**:

Discrete autoregressive modeling, notably through next-token prediction, has become a cornerstone of modern language models. Extending this paradigm to vision allows visual and language models to converge on a unified framework, thereby fostering shared methodology and infrastructure. This work investigates a central question: how far can a finite discrete vocabulary go in representing the richness of the visual world? We present dNaViT, a Discrete Native-resolution Vision Transformer that produces discrete visual primitives analogous to language tokens. Built upon a frozen pretrained encoder, dNaViT applies lightweight residual modulation with a dual-branch objective to inject reconstruction-aware cues into semantic features. By leveraging grouped product quantization, it scales discrete capacity without inflating spatial sequence lengths, while a balanced training recipe maintains both semantic and pixel-level information. Consequently, dNaViT functions as a versatile discrete visual tokenizer, enabling unified encoding across downstream tasks alongside high-fidelity image decoding. Extensive evaluations show that dNaViT matches or exceeds task-specific baselines across 12 visual understanding benchmarks, 4 generation benchmarks, and an image editing benchmark. These findings demonstrate that a compact discrete vocabulary can serve as a universal visual currency and unlock the full potential of fully unified, native multimodal methodology.

### - Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 13 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556)

**초록**:

We investigate the optimal model size and number of tokens for training a
transformer language model under a given compute budget. We find that current
large language models are significantly undertrained, a consequence of the
recent focus on scaling language models whilst keeping the amount of training
data constant. By training over 400 language models ranging from 70 million to
over 16 billion parameters on 5 to 500 billion tokens, we find that for
compute-optimal training, the model size and the number of training tokens
should be scaled equally: for every doubling of model size the number of
training tokens should also be doubled. We test this hypothesis by training a
predicted compute-optimal model, Chinchilla, that uses the same compute budget
as Gopher but with 70B parameters and 4times more more data. Chinchilla
uniformly and significantly outperforms Gopher (280B), GPT-3 (175B), Jurassic-1
(178B), and Megatron-Turing NLG (530B) on a large range of downstream
evaluation tasks. This also means that Chinchilla uses substantially less
compute for fine-tuning and inference, greatly facilitating downstream usage.
As a highlight, Chinchilla reaches a state-of-the-art average accuracy of 67.5%
on the MMLU benchmark, greater than a 7% improvement over Gopher.

### - Zip-NeRF: Anti-Aliased Grid-Based Neural Radiance Fields

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 0 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06706)

**초록**:

Neural Radiance Field training can be accelerated through the use of
grid-based representations in NeRF's learned mapping from spatial coordinates
to colors and volumetric density. However, these grid-based approaches lack an
explicit understanding of scale and therefore often introduce aliasing, usually
in the form of jaggies or missing scene content. Anti-aliasing has previously
been addressed by mip-NeRF 360, which reasons about sub-volumes along a cone
rather than points along a ray, but this approach is not natively compatible
with current grid-based techniques. We show how ideas from rendering and signal
processing can be used to construct a technique that combines mip-NeRF 360 and
grid-based models such as Instant NGP to yield error rates that are 8% - 77%
lower than either prior technique, and that trains 24x faster than mip-NeRF
360.

### - Dense Passage Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 3 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)

**초록**:

Open-domain question answering relies on efficient passage retrieval to
select candidate contexts, where traditional sparse vector space models, such
as TF-IDF or BM25, are the de facto method. In this work, we show that
retrieval can be practically implemented using dense representations alone,
where embeddings are learned from a small number of questions and passages by a
simple dual-encoder framework. When evaluated on a wide range of open-domain QA
datasets, our dense retriever outperforms a strong Lucene-BM25 system largely
by 9%-19% absolute in terms of top-20 passage retrieval accuracy, and helps our
end-to-end QA system establish new state-of-the-art on multiple open-domain QA
benchmarks.
