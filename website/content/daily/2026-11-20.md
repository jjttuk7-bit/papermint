### - Jeveration: System One Models Now Generate

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 120 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24738) | [Project](https://carol-lyh.github.io/Jeveration/)

**초록**:

System One models such as Jev return decisions through fast, parallel prediction. In this paper, we introduce Jeveration, showing that the same decision paradigm can also generate high-quality images in a single forward pass. Jeveration builds on a frozen vision-language model, repurposing its visual-semantic knowledge for holistic image generation. The key challenge is to maintain global consistency. A single prompt admits many valid images, yet parallel predictions must converge on a coherent whole. We therefore condition all positions on a shared random state to coordinate parallel predictions, so that each draw yields a different but coherent image. Since the model tends to ignore this state under direct image supervision, we first learn a distribution over discrete image codes to guide single-pass generation. Beyond text-to-image generation, we further unify image-to-image generation and dense prediction within the same architecture, so that one checkpoint supports all three tasks, each in one forward pass. Experiments show that Jeveration generates a 512x512 image in 78 ms, up to 122x faster than existing unified models, while maintaining competitive image quality and dense prediction performance.

### - ALIVE: Making Streaming Video Models Interactive

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 36 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21366) | [Project](https://lijinzhao30.github.io/ALIVE/)

**초록**:

Streaming video LLMs can continuously observe the world, yet often handle user requests in isolation. We introduce ALIVE, an interactive streaming video model supporting proactive monitoring, streaming task coordination, and feedback-seeking interaction. We construct ALIVE-SFT-288K and ALIVE-RL-3K for two-stage training. Contrastive supervised fine-tuning teaches when and how to respond, while reinforcement learning with Behavior-Aligned Credit Assignment (BACA) combines trajectory-level and local advantages to refine decisions, responses, and silence across multi-turn interactions. We also introduce FSI-Bench to evaluate feedback seeking and subsequent task completion. ALIVE achieves the highest overall scores among the compared models on four public benchmarks and FSI-Bench, demonstrating strong streaming understanding and interactive capabilities.

### - The Resolution Hypothesis: Agent Evolution's Key Is Identifying Improvements

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 35 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23068) | [Project](https://lineshogan.github.io/resolution-hypothesis/)

**초록**:

Agent evolution follows a simple loop: a proposer writes candidate artifacts (prompts, skills, memories) and selection accepts those that score higher. This loop has shown promise, yet practitioners face a puzzle: methods trade wins across papers, and evolution often fails to bootstrap on certain tasks. We trace this puzzle to the Resolution Hypothesis: agent evolution stalls not because methods fail to propose better artifacts, but because noisy evaluations cannot reliably identify them. Across seven evolution methods and six models (over 10M LLM calls), we find support for the hypothesis at both levels where evaluation scores are used. At the candidate level (choosing artifacts), helpful candidate artifacts exist, but selection is no better than random at telling them from harmful ones, so errors accumulate across rounds. At the method level (ranking methods), typical margins of a few percentage points are smaller than the difference between two independent runs of the same method, so published rankings often rest on luck. Together, these findings indicate that agent evolution is often bottlenecked by reliably identifying improvements. This calls for a shift in how we develop and evaluate self-improving agents, from designing ever more candidate improvement systems to making true improvements detectable and accumulable.

### - PROLA: Principal-Orthogonal Low-rank Adaptation for Predictive Spatiotemporal Weather Downscaling

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 27 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11318)

**초록**:

High-resolution weather prediction is important for resolving local atmospheric patterns missed by coarse global forecasts. Large pretrained weather backbones have improved global forecasting, but adapting them to produce future high-resolution fields from recent coarse states remains challenging and costly. We formulate this setting as Predictive Spatiotemporal Weather Downscaling (PSWD), a task that jointly refines spatial and temporal resolution while predicting future high-resolution trajectories. To this end, we propose a framework that decomposes prediction into spatial and temporal stages using a shared pretrained backbone, fixed numerical scaffolds, and lightweight residual heads. For backbone adaptation, we introduce PROLA (PRincipal-Orthogonal Low-rank Adaptation), which allocates a fixed low-rank budget between pretrained weights' principal directions and their orthogonal complement. PROLA further rescales rank-1 optimizer updates using gradient signals, improving adaptation without increasing trainable rank. Experiments in the PSWD setting show that PROLA outperforms representative low-rank adaptation baselines. Further experiments across diverse datasets and tasks support the broader applicability of PROLA, showing that its benefits extend beyond the primary weather downscaling setting.

### - Game2Player: Scaling Gameplay Experience for Visual Agents through Environment-Player Co-Development

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 26 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24154) | [Project](https://game2player.github.io/)

**초록**:

Games are a rich testbed for visual agents, demanding perception, long-horizon planning, and precise control. However, collecting human gameplay is costly, and learning from gameplay videos requires inferring missing actions. Existing datasets also cover a limited range of games, often without explicit tasks or verifiable outcomes. To address these gaps, we introduce Game2Player, a framework that co-develops game environments and players to collect diverse and verifiable gameplay experience at scale. In the Game2Player loop, a Builder agent adapts existing web games, exposes their internal state, and defines tasks with state-based progress measures and success checks. A Coding Player and a VLM Player then attempt the tasks and report problems to the Builder for repair, so that play serves as both environment validation and training data collection. With Game2Player, we build 568 verified web games with 3,436 tasks, collect 15.7K trajectories (307 hours of gameplay), and hold out 50 games as the benchmark PlayerBench. To evaluate the collected experience as training data, we train VLM agents with progress-weighted SFT, assigning greater weight to steps that advance task progress. Game2Player-9B improves progress from 14.3% to 31.2% on PlayerBench and from 30.0% to 53.1% on the external GameWorld benchmark. More broadly, our work suggests a path for visual agents to scale their gameplay experience by building, verifying, and learning from existing game worlds.

### - Efficient Estimation of Word Representations in Vector Space

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 8 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.3781)

**초록**:

We propose two novel model architectures for computing continuous vector
representations of words from very large data sets. The quality of these
representations is measured in a word similarity task, and the results are
compared to the previously best performing techniques based on different types
of neural networks. We observe large improvements in accuracy at much lower
computational cost, i.e. it takes less than a day to learn high quality word
vectors from a 1.6 billion words data set. Furthermore, we show that these
vectors provide state-of-the-art performance on our test set for measuring
syntactic and semantic word similarities.

### - FCOS: Fully Convolutional One-Stage Object Detection

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 0 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01355)

**초록**:

We propose a fully convolutional one-stage object detector (FCOS) to solve
object detection in a per-pixel prediction fashion, analogue to semantic
segmentation. Almost all state-of-the-art object detectors such as RetinaNet,
SSD, YOLOv3, and Faster R-CNN rely on pre-defined anchor boxes. In contrast,
our proposed detector FCOS is anchor box free, as well as proposal free. By
eliminating the predefined set of anchor boxes, FCOS completely avoids the
complicated computation related to anchor boxes such as calculating overlapping
during training. More importantly, we also avoid all hyper-parameters related
to anchor boxes, which are often very sensitive to the final detection
performance. With the only post-processing non-maximum suppression (NMS), FCOS
with ResNeXt-64x4d-101 achieves 44.7% in AP with single-model and single-scale
testing, surpassing previous one-stage detectors with the advantage of being
much simpler. For the first time, we demonstrate a much simpler and flexible
detection framework achieving improved detection accuracy. We hope that the
proposed FCOS framework can serve as a simple and strong alternative for many
other instance-level tasks. Code is available at:Code is available at:
https://tinyurl.com/FCOSv1

### - SentencePiece: A simple and language independent subword tokenizer and
  detokenizer for Neural Text Processing

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 3 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.06226)

**초록**:

This paper describes SentencePiece, a language-independent subword tokenizer
and detokenizer designed for Neural-based text processing, including Neural
Machine Translation. It provides open-source C++ and Python implementations for
subword units. While existing subword segmentation tools assume that the input
is pre-tokenized into word sequences, SentencePiece can train subword models
directly from raw sentences, which allows us to make a purely end-to-end and
language independent system. We perform a validation experiment of NMT on
English-Japanese machine translation, and find that it is possible to achieve
comparable accuracy to direct subword training from raw sentences. We also
compare the performance of subword training and segmentation with various
configurations. SentencePiece is available under the Apache 2 license at
https://github.com/google/sentencepiece.
