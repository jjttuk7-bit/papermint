### - TeleChat to Xing4: Advancing Frontier Agentic Models on Domestic Chips and Frameworks

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 154 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22115) | [Project](https://github.com/XingChen-AGI)

**초록**:

We present Xing 4, a series of large language models that extends the TeleChat lineage through coordinated advances in model architecture and training infrastructure on Ascend. This report describes the design principles and systems engineering behind the series, spanning architecture selection, large-scale pretraining, and reasoning and agentic post-training. We study how sparse activation, attention design, model depth and width, and speculative decoding balance model capacity with computation, memory access, and execution overhead. Our pretraining infrastructure builds on MindSpore HyperParallel, combining topology-aware hybrid sharding, efficient distributed optimizer execution, and coordinated management of long-sequence workloads and activation memory. CPU affinity and core isolation further reduce host scheduling interference. Together, these mechanisms support the pretraining of a 285B-parameter model on 8,192 Ascend 910C cards, providing a concrete setting for the architectural and systems studies. For post-training, we introduce TeleGym, a unified agent execution platform that connects execution, evaluation, and trajectory construction to policy optimization. Disaggregated asynchronous execution, bounded oversampling, and partial rollout accommodate heterogeneous task durations while preserving trajectory continuity across policy updates. Together, these advances establish an Ascend-oriented training foundation for the continued development of Xing 4 across model scales and increasingly demanding reasoning and agent workloads.

### - AscendGYM: A Cost-Aware Kernel Optimization Benchmark for Real-World Workloads on Ascend NPUs

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 91 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17621) | [Project](https://slai-aitp.github.io/AscendGYM/)

**초록**:

Kernel optimization is essential for efficient large language model (LLM) training and inference on Ascend NPUs. Existing Ascend kernel benchmarks primarily focus on kernel generation, with less emphasis on the ability of agents to optimize existing implementations under input configurations drawn from real workloads. To address this requirement, we introduce AscendGYM, a benchmark whose initial suite comprises 48 kernels across three difficulty levels. Each kernel is paired with test cases whose data types and tensor shapes are collected from real-world training and inference tasks. Consequently, performance improvements on AscendGYM map directly to the corresponding workloads. In terms of usability, two evaluation modes are supported in AscendGYM: model evaluation and model-harness evaluation. The kernel optimization capabilities of both trained models and custom agents are allowed to be measured. It provides a complete execution environment that closely reproduces how Ascend kernels are invoked in practical pipelines. For model evaluation, it additionally includes a built-in harness equipped with search orchestration and experience accumulation. Finally, we propose a new metric, the Speedup-Token Score (STS), which quantifies performance gains relative to token cost. By jointly considering speedup and cost, STS enables the assessment of practical kernel optimization capability of a model or harness under an acceptable budget. AscendGYM is available at https://github.com/SLAI-AITP/AscendGYM.

### - Weight-Space Programming Doesn't Need Transformers

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 52 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22055) | [Project](https://wsp-no-transformer.github.io/)

**초록**:

Standard neural architectures such as the Transformer were chosen because they train well with gradient descent and run fast on GPUs. In weight-space programming, a large language model (LLM) writes target weights by hand without gradient-based training, so those reasons no longer apply. Should we keep the Transformer? Given minimal guidance, ten labeled examples, and a sandbox, mainly on multiple-choice questions, we task three frontier LLMs with either generating the weights of a fixed architecture or designing the architecture themselves. When free to design, all three compile plain-text facts into a sparse table queried by a fixed procedure, reaching up to 54.4% test accuracy. An LLM-designed model achieves higher test accuracy than the most accurate released Transformer that the same LLM wrote while using about 1,800x fewer parameters. Without gradients, they choose a table, in which the LLM can state what each weight means and can find and rewrite a wrong one. Each wrong answer points to one row, and removing a row changes about one other answer. From this, we propose an associative-memory-based model up to 17x faster on GPUs than a same-sized Transformer. We also test pretrained weights, other benchmarks, and tasks where models generate answers. Pretrained LLMs remain more accurate; the claim concerns which structure to build when writing weights.

### - Multi-Branch Transformer

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 45 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19800) | [Project](https://gaotangli.github.io/project_page/Multi-Branch-Transformer/)

**초록**:

Transformer language models are commonly scaled by making their blocks wider or stacking them deeper. In this work, we explore a complementary path: branching, the parallel composition of full Transformer blocks. We introduce the Multi-Branch Transformer (MBT), which processes the input through shared initial layers, executes independently parameterized branches of sequential blocks in parallel, and fuses their outputs before shared final layers. At comparable training compute, we study three MBT variants: Wide-Shallow preserves block width, Narrow-Deep preserves sequential depth, and Balanced interpolates between them. In controlled pretraining experiments at four mixture-of-experts scales (0.8B to 2.1B total parameters), 2-branch Wide-Shallow MBTs consistently outperform sequential Transformers under matched parameter and training-compute budgets. Balanced MBTs improve further and retain comparable quality as the number of branches increases. To improve inference efficiency at long contexts, we introduce Joint-KV, which constructs a shared KV cache from branch representations at each depth. Our final recipe combines Balanced MBTs with Joint-KV, supported by an inference engine that exploits branch grouping and shared-cache reuse. Fitted scaling curves indicate that standard Transformers require up to 1.26x as much training body compute to match our final models' validation loss. These models reduce full-model KV-cache storage by up to 34% and deliver up to 51.4% higher decoding throughput on a single H200 GPU. Ablation studies show that branching yields gains beyond changes in width and depth, enables an effective KV-sharing strategy (Joint-KV), remains robust across fusion functions, and performs best in the middle layers. These findings show that branching offers a practical path for scaling language models toward both better modeling quality and faster inference.

### - ForceHarness: Turning Force into Action and Physics for Frozen Frontier Models

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 40 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21099) | [Project](https://showlab.github.io/ForceHarness/)

**초록**:

Frontier multimodal models can now control robots zero-shot from images, yet many manipulation tasks depend on contact forces that images cannot show. Bringing force to an API-only frozen model raises two challenges. First, contact evolves much faster than an API model can decide, so the system must close the loop on force beneath the model. Second, a frozen model cannot interpret raw force streams, so force must be returned in a form the model can reason about. Therefore, we present ForceHarness, a harness that lets frozen models use force without demonstrations or finetuning. After calibrating hand force from a wrist sensor or joint torques alone, ForceHarness addresses the first challenge with self-terminating force primitives that regulate force on their own and return at a contact event. To address the second challenge, a physics translation layer turns each force trace into physical quantities with uncertainty as the tool result. To evaluate ForceHarness against expert force controllers and learned policies under identical conditions, we also build HiddenPhys, a seven-task MuJoCo benchmark with hidden physical variables. On a real arm without a wrist force sensor, ForceHarness raises the zero-shot success rate of GPT-6 Astra from 3.3% to 90.0%. In simulation, it adds 17.5 percentage points on the public ForceBench suite and 34.3 percentage points on HiddenPhys, surpassing force-aware policies trained on demonstrations on both benchmarks and matching hand-written expert controllers on HiddenPhys. Ablations further show that smaller models make significantly better decisions when given physical quantities rather than raw force values.

### - Sequence to Sequence Learning with Neural Networks

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 5 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.3215)

**초록**:

Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) are powerful models that have achieved excellent
performance on difficult learning tasks. Although DNNs work well whenever large
labeled training sets are available, they cannot be used to map sequences to
sequences. In this paper, we present a general end-to-end approach to sequence
learning that makes minimal assumptions on the sequence structure. Our method
uses a multilayered Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) to map the input sequence to
a vector of a fixed dimensionality, and then another deep LSTM to decode the
target sequence from the vector. Our main result is that on an English to
French translation task from the WMT'14 dataset, the translations produced by
the LSTM achieve a BLEU score of 34.8 on the entire test set, where the LSTM's
BLEU score was penalized on out-of-vocabulary words. Additionally, the LSTM did
not have difficulty on long sentences. For comparison, a phrase-based SMT
system achieves a BLEU score of 33.3 on the same dataset. When we used the LSTM
to rerank the 1000 hypotheses produced by the aforementioned SMT system, its
BLEU score increases to 36.5, which is close to the previous best result on
this task. The LSTM also learned sensible phrase and sentence representations
that are sensitive to word order and are relatively invariant to the active and
the passive voice. Finally, we found that reversing the order of the words in
all source sentences (but not target sentences) improved the LSTM's performance
markedly, because doing so introduced many short term dependencies between the
source and the target sentence which made the optimization problem easier.

### - Realtime Multi-Person 2D Pose Estimation using Part Affinity Fields

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 0 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08050)

**초록**:

We present an approach to efficiently detect the 2D pose of multiple people
in an image. The approach uses a nonparametric representation, which we refer
to as Part Affinity Fields (PAFs), to learn to associate body parts with
individuals in the image. The architecture encodes global context, allowing a
greedy bottom-up parsing step that maintains high accuracy while achieving
realtime performance, irrespective of the number of people in the image. The
architecture is designed to jointly learn part locations and their association
via two branches of the same sequential prediction process. Our method placed
first in the inaugural COCO 2016 keypoints challenge, and significantly exceeds
the previous state-of-the-art result on the MPII Multi-Person benchmark, both
in performance and efficiency.

### - Neural Machine Translation of Rare Words with Subword Units

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 4 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.07909)

**초록**:

Neural machine translation (NMT) models typically operate with a fixed
vocabulary, but translation is an open-vocabulary problem. Previous work
addresses the translation of out-of-vocabulary words by backing off to a
dictionary. In this paper, we introduce a simpler and more effective approach,
making the NMT model capable of open-vocabulary translation by encoding rare
and unknown words as sequences of subword units. This is based on the intuition
that various word classes are translatable via smaller units than words, for
instance names (via character copying or transliteration), compounds (via
compositional translation), and cognates and loanwords (via phonological and
morphological transformations). We discuss the suitability of different word
segmentation techniques, including simple character n-gram models and a
segmentation based on the byte pair encoding compression algorithm, and
empirically show that subword models improve over a back-off dictionary
baseline for the WMT 15 translation tasks English-German and English-Russian by
1.1 and 1.3 BLEU, respectively.
