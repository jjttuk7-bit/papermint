### - Turns Matter: Advancing Streaming Video RL via Turn-Level Credit

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 126 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06728)

**초록**:

Streaming video understanding requires models to repeatedly decide whether to respond or continue observing. Recently, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) has been applied to this setting, with existing approaches emphasizing reward design while retaining a single trajectory-level advantage across turns. Such shared credit can reinforce redundant replies after earlier successes and discourage useful follow-ups after earlier failures. We propose TurnWise, a turn-level GRPO framework that aligns learning with individual streaming decisions. TurnWise addresses the mismatch between trajectory-level feedback and turn-level interaction, promoting informative responses and reducing redundancy. Specifically, turn-level credit assignment uses silence-completed prefix scoring to construct group-relative advantages from each decision's remaining return, separating its learning signal from earlier rewards. Turn-mean policy optimization averages token losses within each turn and then across turns, giving response and waiting decisions equal weight regardless of output length. These complementary designs improve streaming policy learning without a learned critic or additional rollouts. Extensive experiments show our method outperforms prior RL approaches for streaming video understanding.

### - From Pixels to Actions - Towards Native Vision-Language-Action Policies

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 57 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06918) | [Project](https://www.lmms-lab.com/posts/neo_vla/)

**초록**:

Vision-language-action models are typically built on a simple premise: perception is first compressed by visual encoders, and action reasoning begins only afterwards. Yet manipulation often hinges on precisely the visual signals most vulnerable to this abstraction, e.g., local geometry, boundaries, depth, and subtle state changes. This raises a fundamental question: should action policies inherit a fixed visual endpoint, or directly access evolving visual computation? We introduce NEO-VLA, a structurally encoder-free vision-language-action framework. Images are tokenized by lightweight patch convolutional layers and evolve jointly with language throughout the backbone, allowing a flow-matching action expert to draw from instruction-conditioned visual representations at different depths. This simple change reveals a consistent pattern. Native architectures outperform encoder-based counterparts under controlled comparisons; full fine-tuning reduces but does not erase the gap, and additional scale alone cannot recover it. More strikingly, layer-wise probing shows that the action-critical signals often peak well before the final visual representation. Across diverse manipulation settings and distribution shifts, NEO-VLA consequently achieves stronger and more robust control. Together, these results suggest that embodied control benefits from access to visual representations across the computational hierarchy, rather than a fixed encoder output.

### - FrontierPhysics: Evaluating Agents for End-to-End Physics Research

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 49 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06910) | [Project](https://www.benchflow.ai/frontierphysics)

**초록**:

AI agents are playing an increasingly important role in physics research. However, existing physics benchmarks only evaluate fixed questions, isolated sub-tasks, or paper reproduction with given solutions, and rarely check research quality alongside correctness. We introduce FrontierPhysics, an end-to-end benchmark with 56 tasks made by 42 physicists based on their own research, covering nine domains across theoretical, computational, and experimental physics. Each task requires agents to review the literature, develop a research plan, conduct research and experiments, and produce structured outputs and scientific manuscripts. Combining deterministic verifiers with expert-authored rubrics, FrontierPhysics evaluates whether agents' research is correct and publishable. Across 22 models in 26 configurations, no configuration passes more than 46 of its 168 rollouts. Manual review of agent trajectories shows that frontier agents' main weaknesses are insufficiently rigorous reasoning and limited expert intuition, rather than gaps in physics knowledge or execution errors.

### - What Does Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning Really Improve in LLM Agents?

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 35 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06483) | [Project](https://rethinking-agentic-rl.github.io/)

**초록**:

While multi-turn agentic reinforcement learning (RL) excels on long-horizon tasks, its performance gains remain highly sensitive to interaction constraints. Evaluating across sampling breadth K and interaction depth T via Pass@(K,T), we show that trajectory-level RL advantages depend on sufficient turn budgets, diminishing significantly when budgets tighten. Besides, agentic RL does not uniformly elevate single-step decision quality; rather, its strength concentrates in error recovery and long-horizon resilience. To mitigate the deployment risks caused by this budget dependence, we provide a preliminary discussion on the benefits and limitations of process supervision signals and introduce BudgetMix RL as an alternative that varies interaction budgets during training. Experiments demonstrate that BudgetMix RL achieves robust performance across diverse execution settings. Project Page: https://rethinking-agentic-rl.github.io/

### - Halley: A Predictive Control Plane for Optical AI Data Center Networks

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 34 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04408)

**초록**:

A frontier AI model is trained in an AI data center, on tens of thousands of accelerators joined by a dedicated network, and every training step ends with all of them exchanging data at once. Between racks, that network is optical, and each of its four layers, from lasers to switches, runs a fixed or after-the-fact policy. Lasers sharing an enclosure, cooling, and power can fail together. At a fixed spare ratio, correlated failure provably caps a pool's availability however large it grows. In this paper, we present a comprehensive predictive control plane framework, called Halley, that reads each resource's next state and acts before the failure occurs. It has four components, each carrying a formal guarantee: LightReserve that sizes the laser pool to provide a coverage bound, FoldFit that schedules the switch within 11/9 of the optimal, ThermaLead that holds a microring with one-step-ahead control, and TriageGuard that protects the highest-risk modules first. Halley gives each layer a predictive controller that defers to the deployed policy when unsure. Composed from simulations at that point, effective goodput, the share of ideal throughput kept, rises from 65.2\% to 76.5\% if a window that exhausts its spares is lost. If only the dark channels' bandwidth is lost, it reaches 67.4\%. Per megawatt, that is up to 1.03--1.17 times the training; for the same training, up to 3.3--14.7\% less data center capacity. Halley's fallback circuits are formally verified and are shown to achieve close timing on a commercial FPGA, and pass a 130~nm signoff.

### - SmoothQuant: Accurate and Efficient Post-Training Quantization for Large
  Language Models

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 6 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10438)

**초록**:

Large language models (LLMs) show excellent performance but are compute- and
memory-intensive. Quantization can reduce memory and accelerate inference.
However, existing methods cannot maintain accuracy and hardware efficiency at
the same time. We propose SmoothQuant, a training-free, accuracy-preserving,
and general-purpose post-training quantization (PTQ) solution to enable 8-bit
weight, 8-bit activation (W8A8) quantization for LLMs. Based on the fact that
weights are easy to quantize while activations are not, SmoothQuant smooths the
activation outliers by offline migrating the quantization difficulty from
activations to weights with a mathematically equivalent transformation.
SmoothQuant enables an INT8 quantization of both weights and activations for
all the matrix multiplications in LLMs, including OPT, BLOOM, GLM, MT-NLG, and
LLaMA family. We demonstrate up to 1.56x speedup and 2x memory reduction for
LLMs with negligible loss in accuracy. SmoothQuant enables serving 530B LLM
within a single node. Our work offers a turn-key solution that reduces hardware
costs and democratizes LLMs. Code is available at
https://github.com/mit-han-lab/smoothquant.

### - DreamBooth: Fine Tuning Text-to-Image Diffusion Models for
  Subject-Driven Generation

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 15 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12242)

**초록**:

Large text-to-image models achieved a remarkable leap in the evolution of AI,
enabling high-quality and diverse synthesis of images from a given text prompt.
However, these models lack the ability to mimic the appearance of subjects in a
given reference set and synthesize novel renditions of them in different
contexts. In this work, we present a new approach for "personalization" of
text-to-image diffusion models (specializing them to users' needs). Given as
input just a few images of a subject, we fine-tune a pretrained text-to-image
model (Imagen, although our method is not limited to a specific model) such
that it learns to bind a unique identifier with that specific subject. Once the
subject is embedded in the output domain of the model, the unique identifier
can then be used to synthesize fully-novel photorealistic images of the subject
contextualized in different scenes. By leveraging the semantic prior embedded
in the model with a new autogenous class-specific prior preservation loss, our
technique enables synthesizing the subject in diverse scenes, poses, views, and
lighting conditions that do not appear in the reference images. We apply our
technique to several previously-unassailable tasks, including subject
recontextualization, text-guided view synthesis, appearance modification, and
artistic rendering (all while preserving the subject's key features). Project
page: https://dreambooth.github.io/

### - Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools

**카테고리**: Survey | **upvotes**: 12 | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)

**초록**:

Language models (LMs) exhibit remarkable abilities to solve new tasks from
just a few examples or textual instructions, especially at scale. They also,
paradoxically, struggle with basic functionality, such as arithmetic or factual
lookup, where much simpler and smaller models excel. In this paper, we show
that LMs can teach themselves to use external tools via simple APIs and achieve
the best of both worlds. We introduce Toolformer, a model trained to decide
which APIs to call, when to call them, what arguments to pass, and how to best
incorporate the results into future token prediction. This is done in a
self-supervised way, requiring nothing more than a handful of demonstrations
for each API. We incorporate a range of tools, including a calculator, a Q\&A
system, two different search engines, a translation system, and a calendar.
Toolformer achieves substantially improved zero-shot performance across a
variety of downstream tasks, often competitive with much larger models, without
sacrificing its core language modeling abilities.
